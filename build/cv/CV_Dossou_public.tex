\section{Job Market Paper}

\textbf{``Pooling Contracts between Venture Capital Institutions''}
(with Pierre Fleckinger)
\\[0.4em]
Venture capital contracts are bargained deal by deal with entrepreneurs, but contracts
between institutions are not: an accelerator committing to a venture capitalist, or a
venture capitalist committing to a limited partner, faces non-negotiable terms and often
commits blind. I summarize my findings. First, when the informed principal reveals the
project's expected outcome \emph{after} the investor has committed --- the LP-to-VC
relationship --- an optimal pooling mechanism exists and the principal's budget
constraint is slack in cash-rich economies, so every profitable project is financed.
Second, when revelation comes \emph{before} the commitment --- the accelerator-to-VC
relationship --- the unique optimal mechanism is again pooling, but the budget constraint
binds in every economy, and some positive-NPV projects are necessarily excluded. Third,
the ordering of these two regimes is what makes the observed uniformity of institutional
terms an equilibrium object rather than an administrative convenience. To arrive at these
conclusions, I set up an informed-principal mechanism design problem in which the party
holding the information also proposes the contract, characterise the incentive-compatible
menus under both orders of revelation, and solve for the optimum under a budget
constraint on the principal. \emph{JEL:} G24, G23, D82, D86.

\section{Working Papers}

\textbf{``Avoiding the British: Brexit and Partner Choice in European
Venture Capital''}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
\item Nothing in the UK's withdrawal restricted co-investment, yet continental venture
capitalists whose pre-determined reliance on British partners was above the median cut
the share of their syndicates including a British investor by 8.2 percentage points
against a base rate of 18.7\% ($p<0.001$), from the semester of the referendum onwards.
The effect is confined to the relational margin: deal counts, stages, round sizes,
follow-on financing and exits are all null, established British ties dissolve no faster
than comparable continental ones (triple difference $+0.3$ pp, $p=0.77$), and aggregate
UK--continental volume tracks the rest of Europe. Among thirteen partner corridors
submitted to an identical, pre-specified protocol, only the British one declines.
Exposure-based event study on Crunchbase, wild-cluster bootstrap inference.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.3em}
\textbf{``MFN Clauses in Sequential Venture Financing''}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
\item A sequential financing game --- accelerator, pre-seed investor, seed investor,
entrepreneur --- analysing the most-favored-nation clause Y~Combinator added to its
standard deal in 2022. The pre-seed investor's equilibrium share is independent of the
amount it advances; the clause makes that investor the agent who decides whether the
accelerator's tranche converts inside the pre-seed block or is pushed into the priced
round, two branches governed by the same threshold equation evaluated at two investment
costs with strictly ordered thresholds; and the clause is weakly dominated for the
investor holding it, strictly so on large financing needs --- a protection granted to an
investor can reduce that investor's own payoff. \emph{JEL:} G24, G23, D82, D86.
\end{itemize}
