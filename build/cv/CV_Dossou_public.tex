\section{Job Market Paper}

\textbf{``Protection and Pricing Power in Sequential Venture Financing''}
\\[0.4em]
In 2022 Y~Combinator began investing on a SAFE carrying a most-favored-nation clause, under
which the accelerator converts on whatever terms a later investor obtains. Between the
accelerator and the first priced round sits an investor who covers an interim shortfall and
chooses both a price and an amount, and the two are not independent: cash advanced beyond
it survives to the round and lightens what the next investor contributes. I summarize my
findings. First, two cutoffs in the intermediate check separate three conversion regimes ---
absorbed into that investor's own block, the two prices coinciding, or pushed into the priced
round --- with strictly ordered financing thresholds. Second, which regime is reachable is
decided by how much the investor advances, not by how the clause is drafted: one that merely
bridges the shortfall never reaches the first cutoff, so the clause hands equity to the
accelerator and leaves the financing threshold intact, while one that pre-funds opens
the regimes that raise it. Third, the clause carries the investor toward that pivot, since
taking a fixed sum out of a block whose size it chooses taxes small checks hardest;
because pre-funding also lowers the threshold within each regime, the net effect is not
signed, and what the model identifies is the ratio that decides it. Fourth, an investor that
adopts the clause it is itself the target of is weakly worse off for holding it. Fifth, I
estimate that ratio: across the 962 companies admitted before the reform the median check is a
fifth of the round that follows --- the corner where the price channel is silent and what
rations is the participation cap it lowers --- and the reform moves seed graduation by
$-0.13$ points, against the $13.8$ by which it fell. To arrive at these conclusions, I model
the four parties as a sequential financing game, solve the conversion price backwards as a
function of the intermediate check, characterise its joint choice of price and amount, and estimate the model by simulated method of moments on Crunchbase accelerator
cohorts. \emph{JEL:} G24, G23, D82, D86.

\section{Working Papers}

\textbf{``Pooling Contracts between Venture Capital Institutions''}
(with Pierre Fleckinger)
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
\item Venture capital contracts are bargained deal by deal with entrepreneurs, but contracts
between institutions are not: an accelerator committing to a venture capitalist, or a venture
capitalist committing to a limited partner, faces non-negotiable terms and often commits
blind. In an informed-principal framework, when the principal reveals the project's expected
outcome \emph{after} the investor has committed --- the LP-to-VC relationship --- an optimal
pooling mechanism exists and the budget constraint is slack in cash-rich economies, so every
profitable project is financed. When revelation comes \emph{before} the commitment --- the
accelerator-to-VC relationship --- the unique optimal mechanism is again pooling, but the
budget constraint binds in every economy and some positive-NPV projects are necessarily
excluded. The observed uniformity of institutional terms is therefore an equilibrium object
rather than an administrative convenience. \emph{JEL:} G24, G23, D82, D86.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.3em}
\textbf{``Avoiding the British: Brexit and Partner Choice in European
Venture Capital''}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
\item Nothing in the UK's withdrawal restricted co-investment, yet continental venture
capitalists whose pre-determined reliance on British partners was above the median cut
the share of their syndicates including a British investor by 8.2 percentage points
against a base rate of 18.7\% ($p<0.001$), from the semester of the referendum onwards.
The effect is confined to the relational margin: deal counts, stages, round sizes,
follow-on financing and exits are all null, established British ties dissolve no faster
than comparable continental ones (triple difference $+0.3$ pp, $p=0.77$), and aggregate
UK--continental volume tracks the rest of Europe. Exposure-based event study on
Crunchbase, wild-cluster bootstrap inference.
\end{itemize}
