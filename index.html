About

I am a PhD candidate in Economics at Mines Paris – PSL, in the CERNA research centre, advised by Pierre Fleckinger and Philippe Mustar (expected 2027).

My work is in entrepreneurial finance and venture capital, combining applied econometrics with contract theory. On the empirical side I study how market-wide shocks reshape cross-border syndication networks, with attention to what such designs can and cannot identify. On the theoretical side I model the contracts that tie venture capital institutions together — accelerators, bridge investors, and limited partners.

Research

Job Market Paper

Avoiding the British: Brexit, Cross-Border Syndication, and the Identification of Mechanisms under Market-Wide Shocks

Solo-authored — 2026

Did Brexit break the co-investment ties between British and continental European venture capitalists? Using Crunchbase data and an exposure-based event study with wild-cluster bootstrap inference, I find that continental VCs with high pre-determined exposure to UK partners durably cut the share of their syndicates including a British investor by 8.2 percentage points; among thirteen partner-country corridors subjected to an identical, pre-specified protocol, the United Kingdom is the only one to move. Established dyads did not dissolve and aggregate co-syndication volume held: the relationship thinned at the new-syndicate margin without existing ties rupturing. When a shock treats every agent on the same date, designs without an untreated unit do not lose precision — they manufacture mechanisms.

Pooling Contracts between Venture Capital Institutions

with Pierre Fleckinger — working paper

An informed-principal framework for contracts between institutions in venture capital. Under ex-post commitment — the accelerator-to-VC relationship — the unique optimal contract is pooling with a binding budget constraint on the agency, and some positive-NPV projects are necessarily excluded. Under ex-ante commitment — the LP-to-VC relationship — the budget constraint is slack and all profitable projects are financed in cash-rich economies.

Bridge Financing under Accelerator Contracts: A Model of Y Combinator's 2022 MFN SAFE Reform

Solo-authored — working paper

A sequential financing game — accelerator, bridge investor, seed investor, entrepreneur — analysing Y Combinator's 2022 addition of an uncapped most-favored-nation SAFE. Solving by backward induction, I show that the equilibrium bridge share is independent of the amount bridged, and that the SAFE's cap acts as a ratchet: on small needs the MFN tranche is threshold-neutral, while on larger needs it degenerates into a tax on the investment cost. The two components of the reform push in opposite directions, so its net effect on reaching a priced round is ambiguous — and therefore testable.
